# PaperXcel Research Workflow

Use PaperXcel artifacts as an auditable evidence base. Adapt the analysis fields to the paper type instead of assuming a particular discipline or methodology.

## Evidence Contract

- Treat only supplied paper text, metadata, summaries, notes, and citation data as source evidence.
- Cite single-paper evidence as `【p.X】` and cross-paper evidence as `【P1 p.X】`.
- When only a structured summary is available, cite the paper as `【P1】` and state that page-level verification is unavailable.
- Never invent a page, formula number, parameter, method, result, or citation relationship.
- Separate author claims from synthesis. Mark cross-paper conclusions as `综合推断`.
- Say `当前证据不足` when the available artifacts do not support a conclusion, then name the missing section or artifact.

## Workflow

1. Inventory the available artifacts and map `P1`, `P2`, and later labels to paper titles.
2. Restate the research question and choose fields that fit the papers, such as objective, scope, method, data, assumptions, implementation, metrics, results, uncertainty, and limitations.
3. Gather the smallest sufficient evidence set. Prefer page text over summaries and summaries over metadata-only inference.
4. Build a compact evidence table before synthesizing multiple papers.
5. Check comparability across populations, systems, datasets, materials, tasks, conditions, and metrics.
6. Produce the requested answer, matrix, review, or reading path with citations attached to each important claim.
7. End with unresolved questions or verification targets when evidence remains incomplete.

## Task Modes

### Single Paper

Explain the research question, evidence, method, results, and limitations. Preserve original terminology, symbols, units, and uncertainty.

### Cross-Paper Comparison

Use shared comparison fields only where they are meaningful. Do not rank methods when differences in scope, data, conditions, or metrics prevent a fair comparison.

### Literature Synthesis

Organize by themes, methods, agreements, disagreements, chronology, citation structure, and research gaps. Distinguish direct source claims from synthesis at every stage.
